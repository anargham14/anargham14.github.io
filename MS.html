Weighing Neutrinos with the Halo Environment
using Weak Lensing Observables

My MS Thesis

Introduction

Nonlinear objects, such as halos, exhibit a scale-dependent bias on linear scales in massive neutrino cosmologies [1], [2]. The shape of this scale dependence is set by the neutrino transfer function, which, in turn, is set by their mass eigenstates, and is not degenerate with any of the other standard cosmological parameters [3]. Hence, this can be used to constrain the neutrino mass, which is an important goal for cosmologists in the upcoming decade. The strength of this signal can be substantially enhanced by using information about the halo environment across a range of scales. This is done by splitting the halo sample based on a combination of the total matter field and the halo field [3]. However, weak lensing measurements from cosmological surveys do not measure the three-dimensional fields, but rather a weighted projection along the line of sight. It would be interesting to investigate the size of this effect for realistic weak lensing survey specifications.

Objectives

This research seeks to broadly achieve the following: (1) Quantify how much of the signal survives when a combination of the projected matter and halo fields is used to split the halo sample, and how much of it is washed away by weak lensing survey systematics. (2) Create a pipeline to utilise the effect described in [3] to constrain the neutrino mass in future weak lensing surveys.

Methodology

The project will use the HalfDome simulations, which include full-sky lightcones and halo catalogs between $z = 0$ and $4$ for 11 fixed cosmology realizations. The simulations evolve $6144^3$ particles in a $3.75 h^{-1} Gpc$ box, reaching a minimum halo mass of $\sim {6 \times 10^{12}} h^{-1} M_\odot$ and a maximum scale of $k \sim 1 h Mpc^{-1}$ [4]. The simulations can be used to calculate the projected density profile of the total matter environment around halos, and the Sunyaev-Zeldovich (SZ) signal, which is a tracer of the environmental halo field integrated along the line of sight. In a similar treatment as [3], we will use a combination of these fields to calculate the bias and investigate the size of their scale dependence.

Expected Outcomes

The proposed research will investigate the usefulness of a theoretically predicted effect (from simulations) in actual measurements, paving the way to utilise novel methods to constrain the neutrino mass. This, in turn, would give us new insights into how much information about neutrino mass can be obtained from future surveys like Euclid, LSST, and the Roman Space Telescope, and would help address one of the most exciting questions in high-energy physics and cosmology today.
